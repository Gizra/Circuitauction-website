Self-Assessment: Where Does Your House Stand?

Answer honestly. Each "no" represents revenue you are likely leaving on the table.

Data Visibility

  • Can your specialists see which lots a registered bidder viewed before they registered?
  • Do you know which bidders opened your catalog email but did not register?
  • Can you identify users who viewed the same lot three or more times?
  • Does your CRM show whether a contact has been browsing categories outside their purchase history?

Consignment Intelligence

  • Can you generate a list of bidders who consistently finish second or third in specific categories?
  • Do you track when longtime bidders suddenly go inactive?
  • Can you identify which categories have high traffic but low conversion?
  • Do you know which consignors' items consistently exceed estimates?

Engagement Timing

  • Do high-intent bidders receive outreach before the sale, not after?
  • Are runners-up contacted within 48 hours with relevant follow-up?
  • Do inactive bidders who still browse receive re-engagement messages?
  • Are specialists notified when a potential consignor exhibits readiness signals?
If you answered "no" to more than half of these questions, your infrastructure cannot support predictive intelligence in its current state.

Warning Signs You Are Missing Revenue

These patterns indicate systematic gaps in how your house recognizes and acts on opportunity:

Consignments Arrive Unexpectedly

When consignors call you instead of the other way around, you have no early-stage relationship and less negotiating position. Predictive systems identify likely consignors months before they decide to sell.

High Registration, Low Conversion

If thirty percent or more of your registered bidders never place a bid, you are not engaging them at the right moment with the right support. Intent signals reveal who needs education, reassurance, or specialist access.

One-Time Bidders Dominate Your Sales

Repeat bidder rates below forty percent suggest you are not nurturing relationships after the first purchase. Predictive follow-up keeps winners engaged and brings runners-up back.

Specialists Spend Hours on CRM Hygiene

If your team manually updates records, cross-references spreadsheets, or searches email histories to understand client status, your systems are not doing the work they should. Unified platforms surface this context automatically.

You React to Patterns After They Have Already Shifted

Discovering six months later that a category is gaining momentum, or that a longtime participant has stopped bidding, means you missed the window to act. Real-time behavioral tracking gives you that window back.

Nine-Point Readiness Checklist

Use this to evaluate whether your infrastructure can support predictive intelligence or requires foundational work first.

  1. Unified client records. Every interaction—registration, bid, email open, lot view, consignment inquiry—updates a single profile accessible to your team.
  2. Behavioral event tracking. Your system logs not just what users purchased, but what they viewed, how long they spent, which documents they opened, and whether they returned.
  3. Stage-based client segmentation. You have defined categories beyond "bidder" and "consignor"—such as evaluator, first-timer, repeat buyer, multi-category collector, and potential consignor.
  4. Automated engagement triggers. Specific behaviors—lot saves, prolonged viewing, registration without bidding—automatically generate appropriate follow-up within defined time windows.
  5. Consignment pipeline visibility. Your team can see which bidders exhibit patterns that historically precede consignment, and specialists receive alerts when these patterns emerge.
  6. Specialist workflow integration. Insights appear where your team already works—in the CRM, the catalog system, or the dashboard they check daily—not buried in separate analytics platforms.
  7. Real-time alerting. High-intent behaviors surface immediately, not in a weekly report. Timing matters. A bidder who spends fifteen minutes on a lot today needs outreach tomorrow, not next week.
  8. Privacy and transparency infrastructure. You have clear policies on what is tracked, how it is used, and how clients can opt out. This is foundational to trust.
  9. Measurement discipline. You track registration-to-first-bid time, repeat bidder rate, cross-category expansion, and consignor pipeline sourced from bidders. You review these metrics monthly and adjust based on results.
If fewer than six of these are in place, you are not ready to deploy predictive intelligence effectively. Start with infrastructure consolidation.

Quick Wins: Five Actions You Can Take This Week

These require no new software, no technical expertise, and minimal time. They will surface patterns you are currently missing.

1. Run a Runner-Up Report

Pull bidding data from your last six sales. Identify users who placed second or third on three or more lots in the same category. Have a specialist reach out to five of them with a simple message acknowledging their engagement and inviting a conversation about their collecting interests. Track how many respond positively.

2. Audit Your Inactive List

Identify users who bid regularly for a year or more but have not registered for the past three sales. Cross-reference this against email opens. If they are still opening catalogs but not registering, something changed. Reach out to three of them and ask what would bring them back. The answers will surprise you.

3. Map Your Favorites Data

If your catalog platform allows users to save lots, analyze which lots are most favorited and by whom. Look for patterns: multiple users favoriting pieces from the same consignor, or individual users favoriting across categories they have never bid in. These are demand signals worth investigating.

4. Measure Registration-to-Bid Lag

Calculate the average time between when someone registers and when they place their first bid. If it exceeds thirty days, you have a trust-building or education gap. Identify the ten most recent first-time bidders and interview them about what would have accelerated their decision.

5. Check Your Response Time to High-Value Inquiries

Review how long it takes your team to respond when someone requests a condition report, asks about provenance, or submits a consignment inquiry. If the average exceeds 24 hours, you are losing opportunities to faster competitors. Implement a same-day response standard and track compliance.

These actions cost nothing. They surface data you already have and identify gaps you can close immediately.

Key Metrics to Track

Once you have the infrastructure in place, these metrics reveal whether predictive intelligence is delivering results:

Registration-to-First-Bid Time

Target: Under 30 days. If this number is increasing, your onboarding and education process needs improvement. If it's decreasing after implementing predictive outreach, you're successfully converting intent into action.

Repeat Bidder Rate

Target: Above 40%. This measures relationship quality. Houses with strong predictive intelligence see this number climb to 60% or higher within a year because they nurture relationships systematically rather than reactively.

Cross-Category Expansion Rate

Target: 15-20% of active bidders exploring new categories annually. This indicates growing engagement and trust. Predictive systems identify these transitions early, allowing you to curate relevant recommendations.

Consignment Pipeline from Bidders

Target: 30-40% of consignors started as bidders. Track how many of your consignments originate from your bidder base. If this number is low, you're not recognizing readiness signals or timing outreach appropriately.

High-Intent Conversion Rate

Target: 50%+ of users exhibiting multiple revisits, document downloads, or extended viewing times should convert to bidders. If this metric is below 30%, your outreach timing or messaging needs refinement.
